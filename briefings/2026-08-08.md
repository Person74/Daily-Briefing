## National

**Bottom line:** A shockingly weak July jobs report reshaped the Fed outlook and rattled Washington's economic agenda, while the Senate opened a fresh oversight fight over COVID-era testimony.

- **Jobs report shock.** July nonfarm payrolls **fell 23,000** versus a consensus **+80,000** gain, driven by a 53,000-job cut in government employment and softness in retail/leisure. The unemployment rate ticked down to **4.1%** — mostly because workers left the labor force — and annual wage growth slowed to **3.2%**, the lowest since May 2021. ([CNBC, Aug 7](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/jobs-report-july-2026.html))
- **Fed bets swing toward a hold.** Futures markets cut the odds of a September Fed rate move from 57% to about **44%** after the report, as traders read the miss as reinforcing a steady-rates stance rather than further tightening. ([CNBC, Aug 7](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/08/07/odds-the-fed-hikes-in-september-tumble-following-big-july-jobs-miss.html))
- **New tariffs on polysilicon.** President Trump signed a Section 232 proclamation setting minimum import prices (**$100/kg** for ingots/wafers) plus a **15% tariff** on polysilicon and downstream solar/semiconductor products, aimed at China's grip on the supply chain. ([White House, Aug 6](https://www.whitehouse.gov/fact-sheets/2026/08/fact-sheet-president-donald-j-trump-bolsters-national-security-and-strengthens-u-s-supply-chains-by-imposing-tariffs-on-polysilicon-and-its-derivatives/))

([CNN, Aug 6](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/06/politics/live-news/fauci-contempt-hearing))
