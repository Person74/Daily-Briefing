# Morning Brief — Saturday, November 28, 2026

*Stocks just had their best week in over a year, but the bond market is pricing a near-certain Fed rate **hike** on Dec 8–9 after October PCE inflation hit 3.8%. That leads because it sets mortgage, borrowing and consumer-demand conditions for everything below, including cruise bookings. The Iran blockade is also escalating and keeping Brent near $95, which matters directly for cruise fuel costs.*

## Markets

**Bottom line:** A broad, AI-led rally capped a holiday week (S&P +1.6% Friday, roughly +5.6% on the week), while futures put the odds of a December Fed hike near certainty. The equity bounce and the hawkish rate path are pulling in opposite directions going into a heavy data week.

- **Black Friday rally.** In the shortened session that closed at 1 p.m. ET on Friday, the **S&P 500 rose 1.60% to 7,297.49**, the Dow gained 1.18% to 50,774.94 and the Nasdaq jumped **2.31%** to 24,129.50. AI and chip names led, and all 11 S&P sectors finished higher ([STL.News, Nov 27](https://www.stl.news/us-stock-market-today-november-27-2026/); [Swingfolio, Nov 27](https://swingfolio.com/daily/us/2026-11-27/afternoon)). On the week the S&P gained about **5.6%** and the Dow about 5.5%, their best weeks in more than a year ([Investrade, Nov 27](https://investrade.com/market-review-november-27-2026/)). *Sources disagree slightly on the close: one puts the S&P at 7,298.34, up 1.62%* ([The Glass Desk, Nov 27](https://theglassdesk.substack.com/p/weekly-market-review-nov-27-2026)).
- **Fed hike priced in.** October headline PCE inflation came in at **3.8%**, up from 3.6% in September and in line with forecasts. Core PCE rose to about 3.3%, faster than expected, and that core surprise is what removed doubt about December ([US Brief, Nov 2026](https://us-brief.com/article/fin-pce-inflation-3-8-october-fed-hike-bets); [Oxford Economics, Nov 2026](https://www.oxfordeconomics.com/resource/us-pce-nowcast-broadening-pressures-lock-in-december-rate-hike/)). Futures now put the odds of a **25 bp hike at the Dec 8–9 FOMC** at roughly 97–99%, and markets price about 62 bp of hikes across the next three meetings ([Market Intel Labs, Nov 2026](https://marketintellabs.com/research/december-fed-rate-hike-odds); [KuCoin News, Nov 2026](https://kucoin.com/news/flash/fed-rate-hike-in-december-almost-certain-analysts-predict-2027-path)). Governor Waller has reportedly floated **50 bp** as an alternative to back-to-back quarter-point moves. *These PCE figures come from secondary outlets and were not checked against the BEA release.*
- **Oil: first weekly gain in November.** Brent settled down **1.2% at $95.44** on Friday and WTI settled at $83.08. Brent still ended a three-week losing streak on worries about Middle East supply ([CNBC, Nov 27](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/11/27/brent-oil-wti-hormuz-middle-east.html)). *Data vendors' Friday Brent figures range from about $92 to $95, depending on contract and timestamp* ([Trading Economics, Nov 27](https://tradingeconomics.com/commodity/brent-crude-oil)). For cruise operators, Brent in the mid-$90s keeps fuel costs a real headwind; see Foreign Policy for the Hormuz situation.
- **Black Friday spending.** Adobe counted **$9.7B** in U.S. online spending by 6:30 p.m. ET, up **12.8%** year over year, and projects $13–13.4B for the full day, ahead of its September forecast of $12.9B. Salesforce, which tracks a different set of retailers, projects $18.7B, up only 4%. *The two firms measure different things, so their growth rates are not comparable* ([US News/Reuters, Nov 27](https://money.usnews.com/investing/news/articles/2026-11-27/us-black-friday-online-sales-to-cross-13-billion-adobe-says); [Daily Shorts, Nov 28](https://www.dailyshorts.io/retail-and-consumer/black-friday-sales-reach-record-high-online-as-shoppers-skip-malls/?post=9872)). Tuesday Dec 1 brings **ISM manufacturing** (consensus 55, prior 55.5) and Wednesday brings ADP payrolls. **Friday Dec 4 is the November jobs report**: consensus is about **90K** jobs, down from 165K in October, with unemployment holding at 4.1% ([CMC Markets, Nov 2026](https://www.cmcmarkets.com/en-nz/market-news/the-week-ahead-apple-earnings-us-jobs-report-ism-pmi-reports)). ## Foreign Policy

**Bottom line:** The U.S.–Iran confrontation at sea is escalating rather than easing. Washington disabled another blockade runner, Tehran vows to break the blockade, and Hormuz remains effectively shut to normal commercial traffic.

- **Another ship disabled.** CENTCOM says U.S. aircraft hit the stern of the Palau-flagged **M/V Pacific Archer** after it left Bandar Abbas and tried to run the blockade. The strike disabled its propulsion, and the civilian crew was unharmed. It is at least the **15th vessel** the U.S. has publicly acknowledged disabling since the blockade began in April, and the sixth since it resumed in July. The U.S. says **158 merchant ships** have been redirected ([Al Jazeera, Nov 27](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/11/27/us-military-says-it-disabled-merchant-ship-trying-to-breach-iran-blockade); [Washington Post, Nov 27](https://washingtonpost.com/national-security/2026/11/27/us-military-jet-disables-commercial-vessel-gulf-oman)). Iran's navy commander says operations to "break the naval blockade" are underway, and its foreign ministry vows to "use all our tools" ([CBS News, Nov 27](https://www.cbsnews.com/live-updates/iran-war-trump-us-threats-diplomacy-strait-of-hormuz/)).
- **Hormuz status.** One tracker describes the strait as **effectively closed** to commercial shipping, with a single transit logged on Nov 22 against roughly 85 a day normally ([Straits.live, Nov 28](https://straits.live/)). Another rates it "Restricted" *as of Nov 26* and notes that Iran said on Nov 10 it would not reopen the strait until its conditions are met ([Hormuz.now, Nov 26](https://hormuz.now/faq)). *Iranian claims that tankers hit mines on Nov 27 have not been independently confirmed.* An Iran–Oman "middle corridor" route has reportedly been agreed but not put into effect, and Tehran says it has **no direct talks** with Washington ([Hormuz Strait Monitor, Nov 2026](https://hormuzstraitmonitor.com/)).
- **Section 301 tariffs proposed.** On Nov 25, USTR made affirmative "structural excess capacity" findings against **14 economies** and proposed added tariffs of **15%–35%**. The 14 are China, the EU, Japan, South Korea, India, Vietnam, Taiwan, Switzerland and six Southeast Asian countries. Vietnam faces the highest proposed rate, at 35%. Rates would be net of existing deal caps, and goods already under Section 232 tariffs would be exempt outside China ([Wiley, Nov 2026](https://www.wiley.law/alert-USTR-Announces-Affirmative-Section-301-Determinations-and-Proposes-New-Tariffs-on-14-Trading-Partners-to-Address-Structural-Excess-Capacity); [Global Trade & Sanctions Law, Nov 25](https://www.globaltradeandsanctionslaw.com/ustr-proposes-section-301-tariffs-on-14-economies-in-structural-excess-capacity-investigations/)). These are proposals only. **Comments are due Jan 5, 2027** and hearings start Jan 25, so nothing takes effect before then. *Sources conflict on China's ceiling: 20% is better supported than a reported 40%.*
- **Ukraine talks stuck on territory.** On Nov 26, Jared Kushner said negotiations have reached an **impasse on territory** ([Ukraine Today, Nov 26](https://ukrainetoday.org/kushner-accused-ukraine-of-delaying-negotiations-due-to-attack-on-putins-residence/)). Zelensky had called earlier in the week for four-way talks among Ukraine, Russia, the U.S. and Europe "as soon as possible" ([France 24, Nov 24](https://www.france24.com/en/europe/20261124-zelensky-urges-four-way-peace-talks-with-russia-europe-and-us-as-soon-as-possible)). *There is no ceasefire; the most recent confirmed development is Nov 26.*
- **American activist detained in Israel.** Israeli police detained **Mel Duncan**, a U.S. activist who has led efforts to protect Palestinians from settler attacks, after stopping a bus returning from a West Bank tour. The grounds for the arrest are unclear ([Times of Israel, Nov 27](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-november-27-2026/)).
