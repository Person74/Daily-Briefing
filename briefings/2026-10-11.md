# Morning Brief — Sunday, October 11, 2026

*Wednesday's September CPI is the hinge for a week that opens with the 10-year Treasury yield at a 24-year high of 5.24% and the Fed having already hiked in September; it leads because it drives rates, equities and cruise-stock valuations all at once. Hurricane Isaias, which hit the Panhandle Friday night, is the other big story but has not affected Miami beyond tide flooding.*

## Markets

**Bottom line:** Stocks closed the week near records despite a yield spike, and everything now waits on the CPI print on Wednesday, Oct 14.

- **Friday close.** All three major indexes rose Friday: the **S&P 500 gained about 0.6% to 7,811.54**, the **Dow about 0.8% to 51,654.95** and the **Nasdaq about 0.6% to 27,366.17**, leaving the S&P within reach of the record set three days earlier. For the week the S&P rose ~1.2%, the Dow ~0.9%, the Nasdaq ~0.6%, while the small-cap Russell 2000 fell ~0.9% ([AP via La Nación, Oct 9](https://www.lanacion.com.ar/usa/how-major-us-stock-indexes-fared-friday-1092026-nid09102026/)). I could not find a single stated driver for Friday's gain.
- **Yields at multi-decade highs.** The **10-year Treasury yield was about 5.24% Friday**, roughly steady after a volatile week ([CNBC, Oct 9](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/10/09/us-treasurys-bond-yields-midterms.html)). Earlier in the week stocks fell as yields surged and the 10-year hit its highest level since 2002; coverage cites inflation worries, expected government spending, Iran-driven oil instability and expectations of further Fed hikes ([BigGo Finance, undated; Sunday Guardian, Oct 11](https://sundayguardianlive.com/business/sp-500-futures-prediction-will-sp-500-futures-open-higher-or-lower-on-monday-october-12-check-us-stock-market-outlook-treasury-yields-bank-earnings-ahead-of-september-cpi-report-302967/)). Higher-for-longer rates are the main headwind for leveraged, capex-heavy cruise balance sheets.
- **CPI Wednesday.** BLS releases **September CPI on Oct 14**. Consensus is **~3.7% y/y headline (from 3.4% in August)**, about 0.6% m/m, with core ~0.2% m/m and ~2.4–2.5% y/y ([Natixis via Kitco, Oct 9](https://www.kitco.com/news/article/2026-10-09/september-cpi-should-be-line-and-last-months-fed-rate-hike-may-be-only-one); [Mitrade, Oct 10](https://www.mitrade.com/insights/more/inflation/us-cpi-preview-december-fed-hike-20261010J1122)). Previews differ on the Fed: Natixis argues the **Sept 16 hike may be the only one this cycle**, Mitrade puts **December hike odds near 70%**, and a core print of 0.3% or more could revive October-hike talk. The next FOMC decision is **Oct 28**, and this CPI is the last before it.
- **Earnings and Fed speakers.** Big-bank Q3 earnings (JPMorgan, Citigroup, Goldman Sachs, Morgan Stanley) start this week, with one preview saying Tuesday, and **Fed Chair Warsh speaks Friday** ([Seeking Alpha, Oct 11](https://seekingalpha.com/article/4953169-banking-bellwethers-kick-off-earnings-fed-chairs-speech-in-focus)). Exact times are unconfirmed here. Royal Caribbean reports Oct 27 (see Cruise).
- **Oil.** Brent was heading for a weekly gain while WTI fell, on mixed signals about possible US strikes on Iran and hurricane-related supply disruption ([Anadolu, Oct 10](https://aa.com.tr/en/energy/oil/us-oil-rig-count-up-for-week-ending-oct-9/60156)). *Price levels are unconfirmed; a figure above $100 appeared in an Oct 1 headline and I could not confirm the current level.*

## National

**Bottom line:** The White House is reshuffling its communications and legislative staff three weeks before the midterms, while Congress pushes back on a Russia sanctions relaxation.

Outlets differ on timing: NBC ties it to a Truth Social post, the Examiner says it was Friday ([NBC News, Oct 10](https://www.nbcnews.com/politics/trump-administration/live-blog/trump-vance-2026-midterm-elections-congress-live-updates-rcna602380)).

- **Hurricane Isaias.** The first Atlantic hurricane of the season made **landfall near Destin, Florida, around 8 p.m. ET Friday as a Category 2 storm**, the first US hurricane landfall since Milton in 2024. At least **4 were dead** and about **480,000 customers** lost power along the Gulf Coast, with emergencies declared in Florida and Alabama; it has since weakened to a post-tropical cyclone moving across the Southeast ([CBS News, Oct 9](https://www.cbsnews.com/news/hurricane-isaias-first-storm-2026-atlantic-season-record/); [CNN, Oct 9](https://www.cnn.com/2026/10/09/weather/live-news/hurricane-isaias-gulf-storm)).
