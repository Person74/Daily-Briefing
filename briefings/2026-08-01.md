# Morning Brief — Saturday, August 01, 2026

*Iran's war with the US is escalating again this weekend — Kuwait intercepted Iranian drones Saturday morning, Trump says fresh strikes are coming "very hard," and the Strait of Hormuz is now carrying a handful of ships a day instead of its normal 130-140. That's the story behind Friday's oil spike, this week's bond-market scare, and the fuel-cost pressure showing up in cruise line guidance.*

## Foreign Policy

**Bottom line:** The US-Iran war is re-escalating, not de-escalating — Hormuz transit has collapsed to a handful of ships a day and Trump is signaling imminent new strikes, reversing the "reopening" narrative from earlier this month.

- **Hormuz nearly shut.** Iran's Supreme National Security Council said Tehran is tightening its declared closure of the strait in response to a US blockade of Iranian port traffic, with Iran-approved transits able to "gradually return" only "once stability and calm are restored." Only **5 vessels** transited in the 24 hours ending 21:00 UTC July 31 (4 outbound, 1 inbound) versus a pre-war baseline of roughly 130-140 per day. Open-source reporting the morning of July 31 described two tankers struck attempting the strait's southern corridor *(as of Jul 31; unconfirmed since)*. ([The National, Jul 31](https://www.thenationalnews.com/news/mena/2026/07/31/live-us-iran-war-strait-of-hormuz-shipping/); [CNN, Jul 31](https://www.cnn.com/2026/07/31/world/live-news/iran-war-trump))
- **Kuwait attacked, new strikes threatened.** Kuwait's defense ministry intercepted "hostile drone" attacks Saturday morning it called "Iranian aggression," hitting a government facility and civilian vehicles with no casualties reported. The same day, Trump told a Camp David Cabinet meeting the US is planning fresh strikes on Iran "as soon as this weekend," warning "we'll be hitting them very hard" — officials cautioned the scope and targets remain unclear and strikes could still be called off. ([CNN, Aug 1](https://www.cnn.com/2026/08/01/world/live-news/iran-war-trump); [Times of Israel, Aug 1](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-01-2026/amp/))
- **Red Sea also live.** Houthi forces claimed strikes on two Saudi oil tankers on July 24 and have fired missiles and drones at Saudi Arabia in the days since, reigniting fears of a wider Yemen civil-war reignition. The International Maritime Organization counts roughly a dozen ships attacked in and around Hormuz since July 6, with two seafarers killed. The UN Secretary-General says he is "deeply alarmed" at the resumption of attacks on commercial vessels. ([CNBC, Jul 22](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/22/houthis-red-sea-bab-el-mandeb-saudi-oil-iran.html); [UN News, Jul 2026](https://news.un.org/en/story/2026/07/1168016))
- **Gaza — real movement, still fragile.** Hamas and other Palestinian factions told mediators (Egypt, Qatar, Turkey, US) late July 31 that they accept a roadmap for the ceasefire's second phase, including staged Hamas disarmament and handover of police weapons to a new Palestinian administrative body, the National Committee for the Administration of Gaza. An implementation mechanism is due within 14 days. Israel has **not** publicly agreed to the roadmap, and Hamas says it won't implement any part until Israeli forces meet their own withdrawal obligations — Gazans quoted by Al Jazeera say they have little hope this holds where prior rounds didn't. ([Al Jazeera, Jul 31](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/7/31/gaza-board-of-peace-announces-hamas-disarmament-agreement-what-we-know); [AP via WSLS, Jul 31](https://www.wsls.com/news/world/2026/07/31/hamas-says-a-deal-has-been-reached-in-gaza-and-other-developments-in-the-middle-east/))

## Markets

**Bottom line:** A hawkish Fed hold sent the Dow to its worst day since April, yields to a two-decade high, and oil climbing on the Iran war — but stocks clawed most of it back by Friday's close, leaving a fragile setup into a weekend of potential new Iran strikes.

- **Fed held, market didn't like it.** The FOMC voted 9-3 on Wednesday to hold the funds rate at **3.50%-3.75%**, with three regional presidents (Hammack, Kashkari, Logan) dissenting *for* a hike — the most hawkish dissent bloc since September 2016. New Chair Kevin Warsh gave an unusually short statement and told reporters "I asked for a good family fight and I got one." Markets read it as the Fed falling behind the inflation curve: the **Dow fell 1,153 points (-2.19%)** to 51,594.14, its worst day since April 2025, and the **30-year Treasury yield hit 5.24%-5.28%**, its highest since 2007. ([CNBC, Jul 29](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/29/fed-rate-decision-july-2026.html); [Forbes, Jul 29](https://www.forbes.com/sites/antoniopequenoiv/2026/07/29/dow-posts-its-worst-day-of-the-year-after-federal-reserve-maintains-interest-rates/))
- **Thursday's data cut both ways.** Advance Q2 GDP came in at **+1.5%** annualized, below the 1.8% consensus and down from Q1's 2.1%, while June core PCE held at **3.3%** (headline 3.7%, in line with expectations, down from May's 4.1%) — a "growth is slowing, inflation is cooling too" print that gave the Fed some cover. ([BEA, Jul 30](https://www.bea.gov/news/2026/gdp-advance-estimate-2nd-quarter-2026); [CNBC, Jul 30](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/30/us-economy-slowed-to-1point5percent-growth-rate-in-q2-june-core-inflation-at-3point3percent.html))
- **Stocks recovered into Friday's close**, the last session of July: the **Dow rose 276.97 points (+0.53%) to 52,485.03** — its fourth straight winning month — the **S&P 500 added 0.7% to 7,489.72**, and the **Nasdaq Composite gained 1% to 25,373.85**, with Amazon shares surging as investors looked past the still-elevated bond yields. ([Yahoo Finance, Jul 31](https://finance.yahoo.com/markets/live/stock-market-today-friday-july-31-dow-sp-500-nasdaq-081227738.html); [Washington Post, Jul 31](https://www.washingtonpost.com/business/2026/07/31/wall-street-stocks-dow-nasdaq/ee08da40-8d1d-11f1-8912-d71e69d679d7_story.html))
- **Oil is the connective tissue.** WTI closed Friday at **$84.03** and Brent at **$87.01**, both up sharply on the Hormuz collapse described in Foreign Policy — Treasury yields have been following oil higher this week as Fed officials publicly argue rate hikes may still be needed. This is the same fuel-cost pressure showing up directly in Royal Caribbean's and Norwegian's guidance below. ([CNBC, Jul 31](https://www.cnbc.com/2026/07/31/treasury-yields-inflation-interest-rates-federal-reserve.html))

([BLS](https://www.bls.gov/news.release/empsit.htm))

## Cruise Industry

**Bottom line:** Royal Caribbean beat and raised again while Carnival's outlook keeps climbing, but Norwegian's guidance cut and rising war-driven fuel costs are the industry's shared headwind heading into the back half of the year.

- **Business — Royal Caribbean Group.** RCL's Q2 adjusted EPS of **$4.21** beat consensus (sources differ on the beat size — PRNewswire's release and most coverage frame it near 6%, other write-ups cite as high as 7.12%) on revenue of **$4.83 billion**, up 6%-6.5% y/y. The company carried 2.4 million guests (+6% y/y) at a 110% load factor and debuted Legend of the Seas in the quarter. Full-year adjusted EPS guidance rose to **$17.73-$17.87**, roughly 14% y/y growth, on record pricing and strong close-in demand. RCL returned more than $600 million to shareholders in the quarter ($199M buybacks, $404M dividends) with $805 million left on the buyback authorization. Shares rose 4-6% in the premarket reaction. ([Royal Caribbean Group, Jul 28](https://royalcaribbeangrouppresscenter.com/news/royal-caribbean-group-reports-second-quarter-results-above-expectations-and-raises-full-year-guidance); [Benzinga, Jul 28](https://www.benzinga.com/markets/earnings/26/07/60731744/royal-caribbean-ceo-points-double-digit-growth-record-pricing-boosts-q2-results))
- **Business — Norwegian Cruise Line Holdings.** NCLH beat on EPS ($0.48 adjusted vs. $0.39-0.40 consensus) and grew revenue 4.9% to $2.64 billion, but missed the top-line estimate by about $55 million and adjusted EBITDA fell 4.1% to $666 million. The company **cut full-year guidance to roughly $1.50 adjusted EPS** and now expects net yield to decline about 5%, worse than its prior flat-to-down forecast — shares dropped 6.3% in the premarket despite the EPS beat, underscoring how much weight the market puts on the outlook over the print. ([GlobeNewswire, Jul 30](https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/07/30/3335864/24500/en/Norwegian-Cruise-Line-Holdings-Reports-Second-Quarter-2026-Financial-Results.html); [Yahoo Finance, Jul 30](https://ca.finance.yahoo.com/news/norwegian-cruise-q2-earnings-revenues-155800304.html))
- **Business — Carnival and the fuel question.** Carnival has raised its FY2026 outlook toward roughly **$2.22 adjusted EPS** and about $7.11 billion adjusted EBITDA, with 93% of 2026 already booked and 2027 pricing running ahead of last year; the company has repurchased more than $450 million of shares and cut net debt-to-adjusted-EBITDA to 3.1x. CCL trades near $27.91 with a $0.15 dividend going ex on August 7. Across the industry, fuel costs are the shared pressure point tied to the Iran war and Hormuz disruption described above — a variable none of the three majors control. - **Ecosystem.** CLIA's 2026 State of the Industry report put 2025 global cruise passenger volume at a record 37.2 million, with 62 ships on order across six shipyards worth more than €40 billion in European investment ([CLIA, Apr 14](https://cruising.org/resources/state-cruise-industry-report-2026)).
