# Morning Brief — Saturday, August 15, 2026

*Israel's public rejection of the US-brokered Gaza peace plan and the still-shut Strait of Hormuz are the morning's most consequential threads — both are live geopolitical risks feeding directly into this week's rise in oil prices and, by extension, cruise-line fuel costs.*

## Foreign Policy

**Bottom line:** Israel's rejection of the Trump-backed Gaza plan remains unresolved a week later, and the Strait of Hormuz stays effectively shut, keeping a geopolitical risk premium under oil even as Congress moves on a new Russia sanctions bill and a 50% Canada tariff takes effect next week.

- **Gaza plan standoff persists.** Israeli Prime Minister **Benjamin Netanyahu** rejected the Trump-backed 15-point Gaza plan on Aug 9, saying Israeli forces will not withdraw until Hamas achieves "genuine disarmament" — reversing the phased-withdrawal sequencing Washington had proposed. *As of Aug 14, Netanyahu had not walked the rejection back*, though Trump adviser Jared Kushner is set to travel to Israel and Cairo next week to push implementation. ([NPR, Aug 9](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/09/nx-s1-5926459/netanyahu-rejects-trump-gaza-peace-plan-israel-hamas); [Times of Israel, Aug 14](https://www.timesofisrael.com/liveblog-august-14-2026/))
- **Hormuz still effectively closed.** Ship traffic through the Strait of Hormuz remains near a standstill — just **1 vessel** transited on Aug 9 versus roughly 73/day in normal times — despite Iran and Oman describing a reopening deal as "close" earlier this month; the strait has been shut since Iran closed it following February's US-Israel strikes. *As of Aug 14; unconfirmed reopening.* ([Al Jazeera, Aug 6](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/6/hormuz-deal-close-whats-the-latest-on-each-sides-positions); [The Hill, Aug 14](https://thehill.com/policy/international/6022634-strait-of-hormuz-conflict-traffic-drops/))
- **Ukraine fighting intensifies, Senate sanctions Russia.** Russian forces pushed into Dobropillia and Kupiansk in eastern Ukraine while intensifying missile strikes, even as Kyiv touts a completed counteroffensive that reclaimed **287 square miles** in Dnipropetrovsk this year. The Senate passed the Graham Sanctioning Russia and Iran Act **86-11** on Aug 7, threatening tariffs up to 100% on buyers of Russian oil including China and India, but the House is on recess until September. ([Al Jazeera, Aug 14](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/2026/8/14/ukraine-liberates-dnipropetrovsk-as-russia-intensifies-missile-attacks); [NPR, Aug 7](https://www.npr.org/2026/08/07/nx-s1-5924914/senate-passes-russia-sanctions-bill-that-was-long-championed-by-lindsey-graham))
- **50% Canada tariffs land Aug 19.** Trump's **50%** Section 338 tariffs on Canadian dairy, alcohol and motor-vehicle imports — the first-ever use of that authority — take effect at 12:01 a.m. Aug 19, with no USMCA carve-out and no expiration date. ([White & Case, Jul 20](https://www.whitecase.com/insight-alert/trump-administration-imposes-50-tariffs-certain-canadian-products-first-use-section))
- **Venezuela talks yield first results.** Venezuela's interim government under Delcy Rodríguez and an opposition delegation wrapped their first round of post-Maduro talks Aug 12, agreeing on judicial reform and access to frozen funds for earthquake relief — the first formal dialogue since the US ousted Nicolás Maduro in January. ([Al Jazeera, Aug 14](https://www.aljazeera.com/news/longform/2026/8/14/talks-between-government-and-opposition-mark-a-new-chapter-for-venezuela))
